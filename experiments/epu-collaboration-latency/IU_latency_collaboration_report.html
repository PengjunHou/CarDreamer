Intention Uncertainty (IU) under Collaboration Strategy and Communication Latency

CarDreamer · CARLA 0.9.16 · Town03 right-turn task · zero-shot evaluation of right_turn_hard.ckpt (DreamerV3). Model-free intention-uncertainty metric. Report generated 2026-07-22.

1. Objective

We study how which surrounding vehicles share their driving intention (collaboration strategy) and how stale that shared intention is (communication latency) affect the ego vehicle's intention uncertainty about the surrounding traffic. Intention uncertainty is quantified by a model-free metric, IU (Intention Uncertainty), computed purely from recorded geometry, vehicle kinematics, the shared intention paths, and the CARLA map topology — no additional model is trained. We then check whether IU is consistent with the ego's actual driving performance (collision rate, success rate, speed) measured on the same runs.

2. Experiment setup

2.1 Scenario and policy

  • Task: carla_right_turn_hard (Town03), fixed ego right-turn path crossing an oncoming car flow. Full observability (the ego always sees surrounding vehicle positions; only the intention channel is subject to sharing and latency).
  • Policy: the released DreamerV3 checkpoint right_turn_hard.ckpt, evaluated zero-shot. The policy is fixed across all conditions; only the intention-sharing configuration changes.
  • Simulator: CARLA 0.9.16, synchronous 10 Hz (1 step = 100 ms). Each condition ran for 1000 steps (~5–11 episodes); per-frame geometry was recorded for offline metric computation.

2.3 Communication latency

The intention channel is delayed by k steps: at time t the ego receives the intention snapshot broadcast at t−k (implemented as a fixed-length snapshot buffer). Vehicle positions stay live (full observability); only the intention/plan content is stale. We sweep k = 0 … 10 steps (0–1000 ms).

2.4 The IU metric

For each frame, IU sums a relevance-weighted residual uncertainty over all relevant surrounding vehicles:

IU(t) = Σi ∈ relevant wi · ui

  • Relevance wi — spatio-temporal conflict between vehicle i (constant-velocity prediction over a 6 s horizon) and the ego's planned path; w = max(0, 1 − dmin/dsafe). Only vehicles whose future may conflict with the ego contribute.
  • Prior uncertainty Uself (entropy) — the ego's uncertainty about vehicle i's maneuver without communication, computed as the normalized entropy of i's plausible routes enumerated from the CARLA map topology (junction branching → high entropy; a single lane → ~0), gated by junction proximity. This is the per-vehicle ceiling.
  • Residual uncertainty ui:
    • not shared: ui = Uself (ego keeps the full prior);
    • shared: ui = Uself · (1 − trust(k)), with a communication-freshness decay trust(k) = exp(−k·Δt/τ), Δt = 0.1 s, τ = 1 s. Fresh (k=0) → u=0 (the maneuver is resolved); very stale (k large) → u→Uself (as useless as no communication).

Design note: an earlier version tied latency to a position-residual γ (distance between the vehicle and the first waypoint of its stale plan). Because that first waypoint is a look-ahead point a few meters ahead, γ carries a k-independent floor that swamps the latency drift, so IU was flat in k (Section 3.2). Replacing it with the time-freshness trust(k) makes latency the direct, monotone driver while leaving Uself (the entropy) unchanged.

4. Analysis

4.1 Collaboration strategy sets the level

IU splits into two tiers: all (10.2) < nearest-2 (17.3) « nearest-1 (32.9) < random-1 (36.0) (×10−3). Strategies that cover the key conflict vehicle (all, nearest-2) hold low intention uncertainty; strategies that frequently miss it (nearest-1, random-1) sit near the no-communication prior. The driving metrics agree: all/nearest-2 collide least, succeed most, and drive fastest.

4.2 Latency only hurts strategies that share the key vehicle

Latency degrades only shared intentions, so its effect appears exactly where the key vehicle is actually shared: all and nearest-2 rise monotonically with k (all: 0 → 18.9; nearest-2: 3.1 → 25.9), approaching the unshared prior as the intention goes stale. nearest-1 and random-1 are flat in k because the dominant uncertainty comes from an unshared vehicle, which is latency-independent. In the driving metrics this shows as nearest-2's collisions emerging only at high latency (k≥8) and nearest-1's collision rate trending up with k. The interaction is clean: latency matters only once you share the right vehicle; if you already miss it, staleness is irrelevant.

4.3 IU is consistent with driving outcomes

Across strategies, the model-free IU ordering matches the success-rate and speed ordering, and the strategies with the lowest IU (all, nearest-2) are collision-free or nearly so. This supports IU as a training-free proxy for how well the ego "understands" the scene. (random-1 is an instructive exception: low collision but the lowest speed and mid success — its residual uncertainty makes the policy timid/slow rather than crash-prone, a different failure mode than nearest-1.)

4.4 Two conditions are both required for a clean latency signal

(i) The freshness formula is necessary: with the position-residual γ, even the simple env is flat in k (Section 3.2). (ii) The simple (flow-only) environment is necessary: in a richer scene with ~12 additional, never-shared background vehicles, a latency-independent floor dilutes and buries the signal (recomputing the freshness metric there yields a noisy, non-monotone curve). The simple env, where under the all rule essentially every relevant vehicle is shared, is the discriminative testbed for both IU and driving performance.

4.5 Full-observability caveat

Under full observability the ego always sees vehicle positions, so intention latency can only corrupt the "which maneuver" information, and even a stale plan can be re-anchored to the observed position. This inherently caps how much latency can move an intention-uncertainty metric. The strongest latency effect is expected in an FOV/occlusion setting, where the ego cannot see the vehicle and must rely on the stale plan for position as well as intention.

5. Limitations

  • Sample size: ~2–11 episodes per condition, single seed. Collision/success rates are coarse; trends (strategy tiering, nearest-2's high-latency collisions) are reliable, individual cells are not. Multi-seed / more episodes are needed for publication-grade curves.
  • Metric assumption: the shared-vehicle residual regrows along the map-branch axis (Uself) as the intention ages; it does not model non-branch deviations (e.g. braking on a straight road) nor the re-anchoring of a stale plan to the observed position. τ = 1 s is a chosen coherence timescale (a τ sweep is a natural sensitivity study).
  • Distribution: the policy was trained on flow-only traffic; the richer environment is mildly out-of-distribution.
